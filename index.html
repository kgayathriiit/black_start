    Live signals

    A simplified teaching model, not the full Simulink model. Voltage = 1 − 0.08 × inverter load and frequency = 50 − 0.5 × inverter load (droop style). Start-up jolts fade with a 0.5 s time constant. The inverter trips above 125 % at once, or above 100 % for 1 s. One second of game time uses one minute of battery.

    The power is about to go out.

    When it does, your home inverter takes over. It can give only 2,000 W. Switch on too much at once and it beeps and shuts down. You have probably heard that beep at home.

    Your job: get all six appliances running again, as fast as you can, without tripping the inverter.

        Same problem, bigger scale

        When a whole microgrid blacks out (a campus, a village, a factory), a large battery plays the part of your inverter. Buildings, solar panels and a diesel generator must come back one at a time, in the right order, without pushing voltage and frequency outside safe limits.

        Our research at the SPEL Lab, IIT Gandhinagar, lets an algorithm choose that order automatically after a blackout: the most load back in the least time, using the most sunlight and the least diesel.
